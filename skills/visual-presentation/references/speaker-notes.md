# Speaker notes

In a self-reading deck the notes are the second layer: the slide shows the point, the
notes hold what a curious reader wants next. They are written for a reader, not as a
script for a speaker.

## What goes into the notes

- the reasoning behind the headline in one or two sentences
- details that would crowd the slide: exact figures, conditions, edge cases, examples
- the origin of every number and claim (conversation, document, measurement, date)
- assumptions and open questions that belong to this slide
- for placeholders: the full image prompt
- optionally a bridge to the next slide ("Next: what changes for the team")

## Format

2–5 short bullets per content slide, in the deck language:

```text
Point: <the headline's claim in other words, one sentence>
Details: <facts, numbers, conditions>
Source: <where it comes from>
Open: <assumption or open question, if any>
Image prompt: <only for placeholders>
```

The field names are a guide, not a must — plain bullets are fine. The title slide may
have a one-line note on the purpose of the deck.

## Rules

- Same language as the slides. Plain words, short sentences.
- No new claims that are not on the slide or in the conversation; no facts without a
  source.
- Never move notes text onto the slide, and never paste the slide text into the notes
  again.
- Full speaking script only when the user asks for a deck to present live. Then write
  what to say per slide (about 130–150 words per minute of speaking time), with the
  transition to the next slide at the end.
